# Licensing / legal notes — Quran Tracker (audit, 2026-10-21)

Researcher scope: third-party content, fonts, APIs, and data processors used by the app; what
their terms allow for a PUBLIC and possibly COMMERCIAL launch; required attribution. **Not legal
advice.** Every claim below cites a URL or a file in the repo. Where I could not find terms, I say so.

## 2. Notes on the highest-risk items

**A. QCF v1 fonts plus a public GitHub repo (HIGH).**
- Nothing I could find from KFGQPC licenses the QCF page fonts. Their own metadata says "All rights reserved", and KFGQPC's site was unreachable from here.
- The only written permission comes from Quran Foundation, and it is conditional:
  - fonts must be obtained via QF APIs or documented CDN URLs (`verses.quran.foundation/fonts/quran/hafs/v1/woff2/p{PAGE}.woff2`);
  - the developer needs an **active Developer Console account**;
  - Quran Foundation must be credited;
  - the files may go out **only inside the app**, never offered separately.
- A public repo with 604 font files is exactly that "offered separately" case: anyone can clone it. The repo also has no LICENSE, so visitors cannot tell which files are third-party.
- Separately, project memory says the fonts were pulled by spoofing `User-Agent`/`Referer` to get past quran.com's hotlink protection. QF Terms forbid attempts "to circumvent technical restrictions".
- QF's terms also do not show that QF can sublicense KFGQPC's rights. A search-result snippet (not confirmed when I fetched the page) said QF's "listing does not grant additional rights" to third-party font files, and to "contact the listed provider directly". Treat KFGQPC as the ultimate rights holder.

**B. Committed derived datasets from quran.com (HIGH for `search-index.json`).**
- `client/public/data/search-index.json` is the whole Quran, word by word, as quran.com `text_imlaei`. It is stored forever, shipped as a public static file, and published on GitHub. Under QF Terms that is:
  - storage beyond 1 week outside Content Sync;
  - a "build-time bundle of QF Content";
  - "Derived Materials that … permit reconstruction of QF Content".
- The Arabic Quran text itself is not anyone's copyright in substance. The risk is **contractual** (breach of API terms leading to revoked access), not copyright.
- `quranStructure.json` (page → verse keys, juz/hizb/rub) is mostly factual and can be re-derived from Tanzil metadata, which lists page, juz, hizb quarters and sajda for the Medina mushaf.

**C. Audio rights for commercial use (MEDIUM, rising to HIGH if monetized).**
- Islamic Network itself holds the recitations only "for free, non-commercial redistribution". For commercial use it defers to the reciters, who "may ask you to remove" content.
- everyayah publishes no license at all.
- For a commercial future, the clean route is recordings with an explicit grant. KFGQPC states (search snippet; its page was unreachable) that it offers its recordings "for their free general use in computer apps, broadcasting channels, internet websites" (https://qurancomplex.gov.sa/en/?p=3979). That covers only the Complex's own recordings, a different set from most of the reciters listed.

**D. Hefz Moyaser private backend (HIGH, operational and permission).**
- There is no public API, no terms and no permission. `newapi.hefzmoyaser.net` is the backend of someone else's product.
- The app calls it per ayah from every visitor's browser for a modern, likely copyrighted tafsir.
- The spa5k fallback exists, but its license status is no better.

**E. Sahih International (MEDIUM; HIGH if commercial).**
- Upstream terms say non-commercial only without the publisher's permission. The app shows the translation with no translator credit.

**Implications for the app:**
- (a) The app itself changes **layout, not text**: `LINE_CORRECTIONS` in `mushafApi.js` moves one word (36:52:11) to the next printed line, with no glyph change. That fits QF's "text … not modified" rule, but the deviation should be documented and reported upstream to QF (developers@quran.com).
- (b) The search index uses *imla'i* (modern) spelling. Snippets drawn from it are not the mushaf's Uthmani orthography, so label them that way in the UI so they are not presented as mushaf text. alquran.cloud asks that reproductions keep "the diacritics and orthography of the Uthmani recension".
- (c) Add an integrity disclaimer plus a "report an error" path. Quranic apps conventionally state the text source and the edition (Madinah Mushaf, Hafs 'an 'Asim, KFGQPC print).
- (d) QF's terms also bar showing content that "misrepresents the Qur'an" or appears next to hateful or extremist content. The AI chatbot should not be presented as giving tafsir or fatwa (out of licensing scope; flag to the content-safety reviewer).

   - History still holds them; rewriting history, or making the repo private, is the only full fix.
   - Add a `LICENSE` for your own code and a `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` that says which files are third-party and **not** covered by your license.
